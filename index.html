$IREN can build a much bigger business and still be a bad investment for shareholders. That's the risk I'm watching.

Monday it jumped 23.86%, from $11.82 to $14.64. Tuesday it fell 11.54%, closing at $12.95.

I own 100,000 shares at an average cost of $17.96. I'm down about $500K, or 28%.

You can't manage a position like that on feelings. You have to follow the numbers.

The business is moving in the right direction.

In the September quarter, AI Cloud revenue was $200M, up 182% QoQ, with an 86% gross margin. Total revenue was $228M, up 66%. Backlog reached $20.3B, up 22%.

Horizon 1 is in service for Microsoft. IREN says its $4B+ ARR target is fully contracted and fully funded, with operations targeted for January 2027, potentially extending into February.

That's real progress.

But a signed contract isn't cash. I want to see the GPUs deployed, customers accept the capacity, and those contracts turn into recurring revenue and cash flow.

Now, how does IREN pay for all of this?

$15B of planned FY27 CapEx is still ahead. About $9B is expected to come from cash, committed GPU financing and customer prepayments. The other $6B is targeted from GPU financing, prepayments, data center financing and other sources, subject to market conditions.

That's not a confirmed $6B funding gap. But a substantial part of the plan still depends on financing getting done.

And equity has already carried a lot of the load.

By October 30, IREN had sold about 76.8M shares through its $6B ATM program, raising roughly $3.7B gross. That's an average gross price of about $48 per share.

Shares outstanding rose from 380.2M at June 30 to 424.4M at October 30, an increase of roughly 11.6%. Not all of that came from the ATM. Mirantis shares and stock awards contributed too.

About $2.3B of the ATM authorization remained.

Here's the hypothetical, not a forecast: if IREN sold all of it at $12.95, it would need to issue roughly 178M additional shares. That's about 42% of the current share count. Existing holders would see their ownership percentage diluted by roughly 30%.

Put that next to the first $3.7B: 76.8M shares raised it. The next $2.3B, at $12.95, would require more than twice as many shares.

That's the problem with raising equity after a major share price decline. The same amount of capital costs existing shareholders a much bigger piece of the company.

Cash flow needs a closer look too.

Operating cash flow was $529M, but deferred revenue increased by $645M in the same quarter. Subtract that increase alone and you get roughly negative $116M.

That's not a normalized cash flow figure. Other working capital items moved too. But it shows how much reported cash generation benefited from customer advances.

Prepayments help fund the buildout, but they also come with obligations to deliver the services customers paid for. I want to see cash flow improve without relying so heavily on those advances.

Investing outflow was $5.15B.

Debt stood at $9.90B against $3.52B in cash, plus $2.26B in restricted cash. I don't count restricted cash as freely available money.

So here's the question I keep coming back to:

Does every dollar IREN raises create more value per share than the dilution it causes?

Revenue can grow. Backlog can grow. The company can become much bigger. Existing shareholders can still end up owning a much smaller piece of it.

I'm still long. AI Cloud growth, major customer contracts and power access give IREN a real shot. I think the market underestimates what this business could become.

But I don't need IREN to become a bigger company at any cost.

I need it to become more valuable per share.

I want to see the $4B+ ARR go live, customers accept the capacity, cash flow improve, and future funding rely less on issuing ordinary shares.

If delivery slips, financing deteriorates or dilution keeps eating into per-share value, I'll reassess. I don't care what I paid.

I'm tracking the business, the balance sheet and the share count. Not just the stock price.

If you own $IREN , follow along. I'll share the numbers that support my thesis and the ones that challenge it.

首先是大方向。即便大盘处于下跌趋势，甚至是一个熊市，在经历大幅下跌之后，也会出现阶段性的反弹。如果连像样的反弹都没有，那就叫崩盘。

上周五6%的下跌，这周一7%的上涨，甚至一度触发熔断。这里面有空头回补，也有闲置资金的初步建仓。无论是哪一种，都意味着市场短期内出现了一定的见底迹象。在前几个交易日上下接近14%的波动之后，最近几天3%左右的涨跌幅，我认为都属于非常正常的局部消化。

所以我现在对市场的观点很明确：我们要看一轮或大或小的阶段性反弹，而不是讨论熊市是否已经结束。而且我的反弹目标也不高，涨个百分之五六我就可以减仓，再涨个百分之八九我就可以清仓。

那么为什么我选择满仓英伟达，而不是其他半导体股票？

如果大家翻看我过去的帖子就会发现，在老黄出事之前，我一直看好的就是英伟达。老黄离开的这段时间，我不再看好英伟达。但随着老黄的回归，我的观点也重新回到了之前：英伟达依然是曾经的那个英伟达，只不过产业链上的一些东西可能会发生变化。

在老黄离开的这11天里，我不认为英伟达的核心业务、竞争优势和盈利能力发生了根本性的变化。面对已经暴跌了这么多的英伟达，股价下来了，估值下来了，但其业务体量和行业地位并没有因此发生根本改变。

英伟达和其他半导体公司不一样，它是这一轮AI产业发展的核心起点。所以在当下的市场环境中，我认为英伟达是半导体板块里面相对最稳、反弹逻辑最清晰的标的。

大盘如果要往上涨，英伟达很可能是重要的推动力量。这里面可以有Mega Seven的集体带动，但未必需要整个半导体板块全面上涨。

所以接下来很有可能出现一种行情：英伟达表现得非常强势，而其他半导体股票涨跌互现。

这就是为什么我做多只挑英伟达。如果英伟达在这么多利好因素的推动下都涨不起来，那其他半导体股票更完了。

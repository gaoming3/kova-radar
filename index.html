PRESSURE AND TIME...

HOW $GME COULD REACH $125 BEFORE OCTOBER 30.

In May 2024, GME went from ~$11 to nearly $49 in less than two weeks.

In January 2021, a violent repricing produced $6.9 BILLION in intraday NSCC margin calls.

Now consider what we're seeing in October 2026.

The convertible hedge appears to be disappearing. Institutional bond buying is returning. Warrant short interest is climbing.

And roughly 59 MILLION warrants are approaching a hard expiration deadline.

I think there's a credible sequence in which these developments reinforce each other.

Here's how the pressure could travel from hedge funds to prime brokers, clearing members, and ultimately the underlying stock.

====================
THE INSTITUTIONAL SETUP

Remember GameStop's August convertible exchange?

$1.4B in debt retired.
55.5M shares delivered September 3.
$358M settled in cash instead of additional stock.

Reported GME short interest subsequently fell by approximately 17.9M shares.

That's consistent with convertible-arbitrage funds using newly delivered shares to close existing short hedges.

Now look at the new data from @101mob and @TheUltimator5.

• GME shares on loan: 135.9M → 54.4M.
• CB2 convertible bond buying has accelerated.
• Institutional-sized bond transactions have returned.
• CB2 prices rose from ~96 to 110% of par.
• The observable common-stock borrow hedge HASN'T rebuilt alongside that activity.

Normally, a fund buying convertible debt for delta-neutral arbitrage shorts common shares against the bond's equity exposure.

As GME appreciates and bond delta increases, maintaining that hedge can require MORE shorting.

But we're seeing substantial bond buying while aggregate share borrowing has collapsed.

That doesn't prove the new buyers are unhedged. Some exposure could be hedged synthetically.

It does suggest a meaningful change in institutional positioning.

Potentially less short-stock supply accompanying the convertible trade, and more directional exposure to GME upside.

And there are still approximately $2.8 BILLION in convertible notes outstanding.

Now consider what could happen if an acquisition or financing catalyst starts the next repricing.

====================
THE $32 TRIGGER

GameStop distributed approximately 59M warrants, including ~14M to convertible noteholders.

Each genuine warrant can purchase one GME share for $32.

They expire OCTOBER 30.

And reported GMEWS short interest just reached 4,296,738.

Here's where the institutional mechanics get interesting.

Suppose GME begins climbing toward $32.

A dealer short warrants becomes increasingly exposed to rising warrant delta. If the dealer remains short gamma, maintaining a hedge may require BUYING MORE GME as the price rises.

Meanwhile, institutional holders with loaned-out warrants may want them back.

Why?

BECAUSE THEY WANT TO EXERCISE.

A fund holding millions of warrants that become deeply in the money has a reason to ensure those warrants are actually available for exercise before expiration.

That can mean recalling loaned warrants.

The borrowers then need to RETURN them.

Some may buy warrants in the market. Others may need to borrow replacement inventory or satisfy contractual settlement remedies.

The warrant can only be genuinely exercised once.

And when multiple participants need deliverable warrants in a shrinking window, sourcing can become increasingly expensive.

This is potentially important because warrant shorting, delta hedging, and loan recalls can all create pressure BEFORE October 30.

No coordinated retail exercise required.

====================
THE OCC SETTLEMENT PROBLEM

Now add OCC Memo #59491.

Since July 30, the warrant component of adjusted GME1 options has settled directly BROKER-TO-BROKER.

Each GME1 contract requires delivery of:

100 GME shares
+
10 GMEWS warrants.

If the delivering clearing member cannot obtain the warrants, OCC can delay settlement or determine alternative settlement arrangements, potentially including cash settlement or a buy-in.

And OCC explicitly CONTINUES MARGINING UNSETTLED GME1 EXERCISE/ASSIGNMENT ACTIVITY.

A delivery obligation doesn't vanish because somebody cannot locate the security.

It remains a financial exposure.

And these bilateral obligations are not fully visible in public CNS FTD data.

We know 4.3M warrants are reported short.

We DON'T know how many additional contractual claims remain from securities lending, rehypothecation, or earlier warrant-distribution obligations.

I'm not assuming they're all unresolved.

But October 30 places an actual deadline on the value of those exercise rights.

====================
WHEN THE BROKERS FEEL IT

This is where the January 2021 comparison becomes relevant.

The first entities under pressure would generally be leveraged funds and short sellers.

As GME and GMEWS appreciate, their short liabilities increase.

Prime brokers demand additional collateral.

Securities lenders can demand margin and recall inventory.

Dealers adjust hedges.

Clearing members face OCC and NSCC margin requirements based on their unsettled exposures and risk.

Those requirements can change DAILY or INTRADAY.

And a fund that cannot provide sufficient collateral may have its positions liquidated.

Its prime broker may become responsible for managing the resulting funding or counterparty exposure.

This is how a trading loss can develop into a brokerage liquidity problem.

We saw that in January 2021.

NSCC collected $6.9 BILLION in intraday margin calls from 36 clearing members on January 27.

In October 2026, there's an additional instrument involved: an expiring warrant with potential delivery and exercise complications.

The critical question is whether firms can source securities and finance their positions quickly enough if GME begins appreciating violently.

====================
HOW THE MOVE COULD ACCELERATE

Here's my theoretical path.

$26 → $32

Institutional accumulation, eBay financing, a corporate announcement, or independent settlement demand starts the repricing.

$32 → $50

Warrants become increasingly relevant. Short-gamma hedgers may need to acquire more common. Institutional warrant holders recall inventory and prepare for exercise. Options positioning may amplify the move.

$50 → $60s

Short-warrant positions become more expensive to finance. Securities-lending recalls and collateral demands intensify. Common-stock shorts begin encountering substantially larger mark-to-market losses.

$60s → $125

This would require the larger common-stock short and derivatives books to become the dominant source of buying pressure.

At $125, the 42.67M GME shares reported short at September 30 would represent approximately $4.2 BILLION in additional GROSS mark-to-market losses relative to Friday's $26.56 close, assuming those positions remained open.

That's before considering hedges or subsequent covering.

Not all those losses would be borne by unhedged funds.

But imagine even a portion of the remaining short exposure being insufficiently hedged when GME rapidly appreciates.

Some firms reduce risk.

Their buying drives price higher.

Other firms face greater collateral requirements.

More positions get reduced.

That's the kind of feedback that can turn an initial repricing into something far more violent.

And it's the larger common-stock book, not just the 4.3M warrants short, that would have to sustain a move toward $125.

====================
WHY OCTOBER COULD MATTER

I've spent months comparing the historical GME pressure cycles.

Jan. 13 → Jan. 27, 2021: 10 trading sessions.

May 2 → May 16, 2024: 11 trading sessions.

Sept. 9 → Sept. 23, 2026: 11 trading sessions.

My preferred starting window for Round 2 remains OCTOBER 9–13.

Another 10–11-session run would bring us into the October 23–28 neighborhood.

Just before the October 30 warrant expiration.

October 16 monthly OpEx sits directly in the middle.

And if GME appreciates meaningfully before expiration, GameStop could receive up to approximately $1.9B in gross proceeds from full cash exercise.

Those exercises also issue new shares, which could relieve some short-delivery pressure.

The race is whether the new supply and available financing can accommodate the hedging, settlement, and short-covering demand.

====================
THE BIG PICTURE

JANUARY 2021:
Violent repricing → clearinghouse collateral shock → brokerage liquidity stress.

MAY 2024:
Early buying → rapid repricing → intense derivatives activity.

OCTOBER 2026:
Convertible hedge unwind → renewed institutional bond buying → warrant delta and recalls → expiration-related settlement pressure → potential collateral escalation.

A move to $125 would be an extreme squeeze outcome. It requires a genuine catalyst and substantial net buying, not merely warrants expiring.

But the institutional setup offers several mechanisms through which an initial move could become self-reinforcing.

And the most compelling part is that much of this could happen without retail initiating it.

Convertible funds.
Warrant lenders.
Short sellers.
Options dealers.
Prime brokers.
Clearing members.

Different positions. Different obligations.

But all responding to the same rapidly changing underlying price.

The next institution that needs to reduce exposure may be buying from the institution that just drove the price higher.

That's the kind of market structure that interests me.

May 2024 demonstrated how quickly GME could move when concentrated buying overwhelmed liquidity.

January 2021 demonstrated the consequences when volatility reached the clearing system.

October 2026 offers a potentially unusual combination of BOTH.

September was the first pocket.

October 9–13 remains my proposed starting window for ROUND 2.

And the warrants expire in less than three weeks.

PRESSURE AND TIME.

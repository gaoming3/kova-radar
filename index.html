This selloff in memory stocks makes absolutely NO sense to me.

$MU, $SNDK, Samsung and SK Hynix are getting hit after China's CXMT announced progress on its new 4F² DRAM architecture, with plans to introduce DDR5 server memory using the technology by the end of 2026. For those unfamiliar, this technology is designed to pack more memory cells into the same amount of space, potentially improving chip density and manufacturing efficiency.

Now, I want to make something clear. CXMT is absolutely becoming a legitimate competitor because acording to TrendForce, CXMT has already captured roughly 9.5% of global DRAM revenue, up from 7.6% the previous quarter, making it the fourth largest DRAM manufacturer globally. The company is also expanding aggressively, with new manufacturing facilities in Shanghai and Hefei targeting more than 600,000 wafers per month, potentially doubling its manufacturing capacity. On top of that, CXMT has reportedly started mass production of its G5 DRAM platform and LPDDR6 memory, while its new 4F² architecture could make memory production even more efficient.

But here's why I think the market is getting WAY ahead of itself. First, announcing a new memory architecture and successfully manufacturing it at scale are two completely different things. CXMT still needs to prove it can deliver competitive yields, pricing, reliability, and production volumes. Second, we're currently dealing with an enormous memory supply shortage, particularly as hyperscalers continue increasing AI infrastructure spending. Even if CXMT successfully doubles its capacity, that doesn't automatically mean the industry becomes oversupplied. Demand for memory is expected to grow substantially over the next several years, and we're already seeing manufacturers secure longer term supply agreements with customers.

Third, this announcement is primarily focused on conventional DDR5 server memory, NOT HBM. That's an important distinction because HBM is becoming one of the most valuable parts of the memory industry, with Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix benefiting from enormous AI demand. And SanDisk primarily manufactures NAND flash memory, not DRAM. So selling $SNDK specifically because of a DDR5 technology announcement seems even more questionable. Now, that doesn't mean CXMT won't eventually put pressure on conventional DRAM prices. Increased Chinese manufacturing capacity is absolutely something investors should monitor, especially over the longer term but I think investors are confusing a legitimate long term competitive threat with an immediate change in industry fundamentals. Nothing about today's announcement suddenly creates millions of additional memory chips, eliminates existing supply shortages, or changes the amount of memory needed to power AI infrastructure. I've been saying this for months. The amount of memory we're going to need over the next decade is INSANE. Are you willing to buy a stock when others are selling and it's UGLY?

Example...

At 3 o’clock in the morning, most of the country is asleep.

On July 17th, @epictrades1 was half-awake, placing a buy order in $MU at 812.

It wasn’t because he’s addicted to action. He saw the opportunity forming the night before.

Rule #1 shared yesterday was to trade the levels...not the money.

His Rule #2: Buy the most where it feels the worst.

Most traders put their biggest size in where it feels best.

The chart is clean, the stock is green, and everybody on CNBC agrees.

Then the stock drops 3 percent and they’re stuck, because they spent all their ammunition where they had the least edge.

“I’m down 3%, maybe I should take a stop loss, that’s what seems prudent.”

You book the 3% loss, tell yourself you did a good job, and all you did was reinforce a bad habit.

David does it backwards.

Wherever he thinks the best price is, meaning the most downside on a long, that’s where he buys the most.

If he’s buying 1,000 shares at 840 and it drops to 820, he’s often buying 3,000 there.

In his words: “Otherwise, how does your cost ever get decent?”

Three things make this work:

→ He decides his full size before the first buy. The first piece is a starter, maybe a quarter of what he wants to own. The bulk waits for his level.

→ He expects to be down on the first piece. When it drops to his add level, that’s not an “oh my gosh, I’m down” level. It’s an “ooh, I sense an opportunity” level.

→ He never buys something just because it’s down. Down is not a reason. That’s how you end up bag-holding $ETSY since 2021.

Here’s what that looked like with Micron at 3AM.

Micron wasn’t even oversold. RSI was around 39 or 40 and could easily have gone into the 20s.

He didn’t buy it because the chart looked good or bad.

He bought it because Micron had just reported a strong quarter, and the drop was way out of proportion to what the company was earning.

The room had been pointing to the 820 area for days. At 3AM that Friday, his order was sitting at 812.

Between 4 and 5AM, before the market even opened, Micron bounced about 50 points.

He sold pieces into strength at 830, then 840.

Then at the 9:30 open, Micron dropped back under his 3AM buy price.

Most traders would panic sell. Say “I’m wrong,” take the stop, call themselves a smart trader and leave.

David did the opposite. He remembered his levels and why he got into the stock in the first place.

He bought bigger on the dip.

Within 60 minutes, the stock bounced nearly 70 points. He trimmed to a trailer and held the rest up to 900.

All before lunch.

Now here’s the wrong way to do it.

Same week, David gave the room an example.

Say you’re mad every time the market drops because you never buy enough. So this time you swing big. $QQQ was at 725 that morning, and you buy 4,000 shares at 702. All of it, in one clip.

“I’m buying the dip,” you say.

It bounces to 703. You get nervous and sell 1,500.

Congrats, you made 0.10% on your book. That’s cat food money.

Then the Qs reverse. 698… 695… 690…

By 686, you’ve puked it and destroyed your month.

That’s the exact same 686 level David called on the Qs a week early.

At 686, one trader had planned for the pain and was buying the most. The other got ambushed by it and was selling everything.

Another trader told David he’d made money on the bounce. Turns out he’d bought early, got scared, then felt better once it came back.

David: “You made 40 bucks, and you were down 40 bucks. That’s not amazing.”

Amazing is when you own a little, wait for your level, buy more when it gets ugly, and your reward ends up three or four times your risk.

Buy ugly.

$ASTS: 🚨 DEUTSCHE BANK UPGRADES AST SPACEMOBILE TO BUY FROM HOLD WITH $93 PRICE TARGET

Upgrading to Buy
We are upgrading ASTS to Buy and maintaining our $93 PT. The stock is down ~65% from its all-time high in May. We had downgraded the stock in May after the New Glenn launchpad explosion that introduced a material delay in ASTS' launch plans, combined with valuation concerns. However, we believe now is an opportune time to become more constructive given the recent stock-price weakness, a number of positive developments/catalysts that we think have gone under the radar, and spectrum value that we estimate now comprises all but $10 per share in ASTS' EV. Furthermore, we think the -10% move on Friday was misguided since the 800 MHz spectrum SpaceX will be acquiring doesn't make SpaceX more competitive with ASTS as an MNO partner for D2D, and ASTS isn't targeting direct-to-consumer (for which SpaceX will primarily use the 800 MHz).

We see a number of positive developments/catalysts for ASTS in the near term:

➡️ We believe it is increasingly likely that T-Mobile will switch from Starlink to ASTS as its D2D partner (through the JV). Roughly coincident with the timing of the announcement that AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile reached a definitive agreement to form a D2D JV on Oct. 1; T-Mobile appears to have removed many (but not all) references to Starlink from its website. Furthermore, SpaceX's efforts to compete with the Big 3 MNOs in mobile continues to antagonize the Big 3 (and shrink their market capitalizations), pushing them even closer to ASTS. SpaceX's announced purchase of 14 MHz of 800 MHz spectrum from Grain Management on October 8 was the latest shot across the bows of the Big 3, leading to a 10-13% decline in their stock prices on Friday (just the latest SpaceX-driven market-cap destruction for the telcos).

➡️ The Space Development Agency (SDA) sent a letter to the National Telecommunications and Information Administration (to be then forwarded to the FCC) in September expressing support for ASTS' request for Temporary Authority to conduct testing of pulsed radiolocation capabilities in the 902–928 MHz band using its in-orbit BlueBird satellites. The letter names ASTS as a subcontractor and stresses the national security importance of testing these capabilities (which appear to be sensing related). We believe this is indicative of continued progress by ASTS in expanding its government contract base, a revenue opportunity that seems to be expanding every day.

➡️ The preliminary selection of ASTS (along with Rakuten) for the Japan LEO project took a step forward when the October 5 Joint Statement on the U.S.-Japan Technology Prosperity Deal specifically "welcomed Japan's Rakuten Group and the U.S.-based AST SpaceMobile cooperation for the development and deployment of Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite infrastructure". We view this as a sign that the official J-LEO authorization could be coming soon. If/when approved, it means the Government of Japan will fund up to $1B of capex for ASTS' satellite constellation in exchange for sovereign use and control of a number of satellites while they are able to provide coverage to Japan. This "up to $1B" is not reflected in our current valuation, even though it would represent essentially free capital, significantly improving ASTS' liquidity position.

Underlying Spectrum Value Means The Value of The Business Itself Is Highly Discounted

ASTS' EV is currently $23B, while we estimate the spectrum rights ASTS has through its long-term lease with Ligado represent $19B in value, implying only a $4B valuation for the rest of the company, or $10 per share. This assumes $1.09 per MHz*POP, equal to the price SpaceX agreed to pay Echostar in that transaction signed in September 2025.

We Also See Optionality From Potential Industry Consolidation

We believe ASTS could be of significant strategic value to a larger company with LEO and D2D ambitions given its spectrum position, novel satellite architecture, IP/patent portfolio, spectrum rights, MNO partnerships, engineering talent, manufacturing capabilities/facilities, and nascent government business.

Launch Is Still A Challenge, But Looks Incrementally Better Today

Our view has been for some time that securing launch and putting satellites into orbit is the most critical success factor for ASTS over the next couple years because it would facilitate commercial-service introduction and begin to accelerate revenue growth. It is also important defensively to ensure MNO partners stick with ASTS and the company's government-contract base expands. The launch bottleneck continues to be a challenge. However, Blue Origin seems to be making encouraging progress toward rebuilding the launch pad destroyed earlier this year (potentially launching again before YE), and ASTS is prepared to pay a premium for additional launch from SpaceX and others to help mitigate the impact of less Blue Origin launch availability. Thus, while we continue to have concerns regarding launch availability; (1) we think the picture is incrementally positive relative to a quarter ago given Blue Origin's progress, and (2) we think these other aforementioned catalysts will define the narrative between now and the end of the year, since the industry launch-capacity situation is already fairly well appreciated by the market.

THE DATA LAYER PHYSICAL AI NEEDS TO UNDERSTAND THE REAL WORLD

Robotics, autonomous systems, simulation, and digital twins all depend on accurate representations of physical environments. Yet collecting ground-level spatial data at scale remains a challenge, especially when teams need specific locations and verifiable data origins.

@vangrid_io is approaching this through decentralized spatial data collection, 3D reconstruction, and cryptographic provenance.

Here is how the system connects the physical world to usable digital assets.

→ Ground-Level Data Collection: Contributors capture real environments using accessible capture tools, helping build spatial datasets for mapping, robotics, and physical AI applications.

→ 3D Reconstruction: Captured environments can be reconstructed into textured 3D meshes in GLB format, giving developers assets they can integrate into compatible workflows.

→ Verifiable Provenance: Cryptographic fingerprints connect captures to their source data, making provenance easier to verify instead of relying entirely on undocumented uploads.

→ Commissioned Capture Bounties: When a required location is missing, teams can specify what they need, fund a bounty, review submissions, and accept a suitable capture. Reconstruction is included.

→ Enterprise Data Access: Organizations can explore available spatial datasets through Vangrid’s data marketplace, with regional data routing options for supported deployments.

The interesting part is the connection between data collection and actual demand.

Instead of relying exclusively on existing datasets, teams can commission captures of specific places. Contributors collect the missing ground truth, and reconstruction converts those recordings into usable spatial assets.

That creates a potential feedback loop between real-world data demand, contributor participation, and the infrastructure needed to support physical AI.

The opportunity extends beyond mapping. Reliable spatial data can support environment reconstruction, autonomous navigation, robotics training, and simulation, provided the data meets each application’s accuracy and coverage requirements.

Physical AI needs more than models that understand the world in theory. It needs useful data about the world as it actually exists.

That is the infrastructure layer $VAN is working to build.

目前来看，有三点值得关注。

1.$ACU 经济模型是否合理？

2. Acurast 是否实现稳定营收？

3.如何避免被工作室或者科学家薅羊毛？

════════════════

经济模型 通胀支撑启动，质押与销毁形成对冲

Acurast 初始供应 10 亿枚 ACU，年度固定通胀 5%，治理可调整。通胀分配中，70% 进入 Staked Compute Pool，也就是质押算力池，10% 给基础 Benchmark 奖励，即 Compute Pool，15% 进金库，5% 给 Collators。

这是典型的通胀换参与设计，目的是在早期吸引手机节点和质押者。

质押机制是核心约束。处理器运营者需要承诺算力并质押 ACU。

另外，部署执行费用在处理器成功上报后会被销毁，从部署预算中扣除，形成直接的代币消耗。

从设计上看，这个模型比纯通胀挖矿更有约束力。长期冷却和 slash 能降低短期抛压，销毁则试图把真实使用转化为通缩压力。但它的合理性最终取决于销毁能否跟上或接近通胀。

目前公开信息里，销毁规模的透明追踪还不够充分，市场仍主要按解锁加通胀定价。如果付费部署的消耗持续放大，这个平衡会改善。如果消耗长期偏低，通胀和陆续解锁就会持续压制价格。

════════════════

营收情况 部署量在涨，但付费规模仍需验证

网络侧数据看起来不错。手机节点已超 30 万台，覆盖 170 多个国家，链上交易突破 10 亿，累计部署也从早期的十几万涨到 60 万以上。

官方多次强调部署是真实工作负载，包括 API、Webhook、机密计算，以及近期的 AI 推理，比如 Laya 模型在手机上运行。定价采用网络派生方式，而非节点自行报价，执行费被销毁，节点主要靠通胀奖励加忙碌加成 Busy Bonus 获益。

这些数字说明供给端和编排层在增长，也有部分真实用例，例如预言机相关合作。但部署数量不等于付费营收规模。链上交易中包含大量心跳、确认和上报，付费执行占比缺乏持续公开的拆分数据。

对 DePIN 来说，稳定营收的标志是开发者持续支付的计算费用能够形成可观的代币消耗，并逐步覆盖或对冲通胀。目前这个闭环还在形成中，网络活跃，但代币价格与使用量之间的传导还不够直接。

如果付费部署和销毁量持续上升，经济模型会更健康。如果主要靠激励维持节点，价格对解锁和抛压会更敏感。这是目前最需要关注的指标。

════════════════

抗薅羊毛 硬件验证加质押 slash 抬高门槛

DePIN 最怕的是工作室批量上设备或模拟器刷量。

Acurast 的主要防线是硬件层面的持续验证。依赖手机 TEE 和厂商 Attestation，比如 Android 的 Key Attestation、iOS 的 App Attest，确认设备真实、系统未篡改。团队还持续监控并移除伪造或被撤销的 Attestation，模拟器和脚本心跳难以长期存活。

社区里确实存在手机农场，从几十台到上百台的规模化运营。但这些农场仍需要真实设备、电力、网络和质押成本，门槛比纯脚本或模拟器高。完全杜绝工作室不现实，但机制把低成本无限复制的空间压得很小，更多是有成本的规模化供给，而不是无成本刷量。

════════════════

能否活得更长

挖提卖循环在 DePIN 里很常见，Acurast 不是免疫的。

它的优势在于，质押冷却拉长退出时间，slash 和销毁提供消耗与惩罚，硬件验证抬高刷量成本，以及部署量的持续增长为付费需求留出空间。

劣势也很明确，5% 通胀与逐步解锁仍在，付费销毁的规模和透明度还需要更多数据支撑，价格对供给端更敏感。

上更多交易所能改善流动性和触达，但无法替代真实使用。

对 Acurast 来说，能活更久的关键不是短期奖池或单次上所，而是付费部署的消耗能否逐渐与通胀和抛压形成更平衡的关系。

目前看，机制上有对冲设计，供给端有规模，但需求端的货币化还需要时间验证。持续跟踪销毁量、付费部署占比和解锁节奏，比盯着单日价格更有参考价值。

Look at what these companies are expected to earn and what investors are paying for those earnings at the prices in this chart:

$NVDA: EPS growing from $9.29 in FY27 to $21.38 in FY29. That’s 130% growth, with shares trading at 22x FY27 earnings and 9.6x FY29 estimates.

$AVGO: EPS growing from $11.68 in FY26 to $30.63 in FY28. That’s 162% growth, at just 10.6x FY28 estimates.

$ORCL: EPS growing from $8.14 in FY27 to $15.71 in FY29. That’s 93% growth, at 8.3x FY29 estimates.

$MSFT: EPS growing from $20.01 in FY27 to $29.50 in FY29. That’s 47% growth, at 17.7x FY29 estimates.

$META: EPS growing from $29.18 in FY26 to $40.03 in FY28. That’s 37% growth, at 15.4x FY28 estimates.

Those growth figures cover two fiscal years. They aren’t annual growth rates.

I have a hard time calling that a broad bubble. Several of these businesses are expected to deliver substantial earnings growth while trading at valuations that give investors a reasonable argument for owning them.

Of course consensus estimates aren’t guarantees. A low multiple on earnings that never materialize won’t protect anybody. These companies still have to execute and the spending on AI has to produce an economic return.

That’s where I believe the debate should be and anyone who this that the market is in trouble should be articulating why these earnings won't materialize.

Tell me why the earnings estimates are wrong. Tell me where demand breaks down, margins deteriorate, or customers stop spending. A red week on the screen doesn’t answer any of those questions.

I’m also not saying every company in this chart is cheap. There are meaningful differences and buying anything with “AI” attached to it is not an investment strategy.

For someone investing over the next three to five years this drawdown looks a lot more like an early holiday sale than evidence that the whole sector is a bubble.

If the earnings outlook holds up while share prices fall, I’m being offered those expected profits at a lower price. That deserves a closer look.

I don’t need to catch the exact bottom. I need to be comfortable with the business, the price I’m paying, and its ability to grow earnings over time.

Looking at these estimates, I see plenty of reasons to keep shopping.

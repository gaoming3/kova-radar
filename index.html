$NBIS just delivered one of its strongest quarters ever, yet the stock is getting crushed. And I think the reason behind the selloff is actually more interesting than the earnings themselves.

First, let’s look at the numbers. Revenue surged 571% year over year to $980.8 million, comfortably beating expectations of $908 million. Adjusted EBITDA came in at $465.8 million versus estimates of $353 million, while revenue grew another 68% from the previous quarter. Annualized recurring revenue reached $6.4 billion, and the company ended September with $7.4 billion in cash.

What’s even more impressive is what’s happening with demand. Nebius has secured more than $12 billion in new contracts since the beginning of Q3, while GPU pricing increased by over 20% sequentially. Revenue contracted per megawatt also climbed from roughly $20 million to more than $25 million, and estimated GPU payback periods improved from 22 months to 19 months. Customers are willing to pay substantially more to secure AI computing capacity, while Nebius is recovering its infrastructure investments faster. That’s a pretty encouraging development for the entire neocloud industry, especially considering how much money these companies are spending on GPUs and data centers.

But here’s where the problems begin.

Despite beating earnings expectations, Nebius lowered its 2026 revenue outlook toward the bottom of its previous $3 billion to $3.4 billion range. Some of the computing capacity intended for Microsoft has been delayed, pushing expected revenue into 2027. The issue appears to involve power infrastructure and permitting problems at facilities operated by third-party partner DataOne. Temporary generators were taken offline before enough replacement fuel cell capacity was available, creating delays in bringing additional computing capacity online.

And this is exactly why investors are concerned. Nebius has the customers, the contracts, and the demand, but it’s having trouble delivering the infrastructure quickly enough to recognize that revenue. When you’re growing this aggressively, even relatively small construction or power-related setbacks can have a substantial impact on financial projections. To make matters worse, the company increased its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to $27 billion, compared with its previous range of $20 billion to $25 billion. So Nebius is now expecting to spend more money while generating less near-term revenue than initially anticipated. That’s not exactly the combination investors want to see.

Looking ahead, management expects revenue to reach $12 billion to $13 billion in 2027, representing roughly four times its anticipated 2026 revenue. However, getting there will require another $40 billion to $45 billion in capital expenditures. That’s an extraordinary amount of money for a company currently generating less than $1 billion in quarterly revenue.

One encouraging development is how Nebius plans to fund this expansion. Customer prepayments covered the associated capital expenditures for its Q3 deals, compared with approximately 50% to 60% in Q2. Management also expects customer prepayments and financing secured against existing contracts to cover around 75% to 85% of its 2027 spending.
This could substantially reduce the amount of additional capital Nebius needs to raise through traditional financing, although it doesn’t eliminate debt obligations or the risk of shareholder dilution. Management is also targeting pre-tax breakeven by the end of 2027, but investors should remember that accounting profitability and positive free cash flow are two very different things when a company is spending tens of billions of dollars annually.

Personally, I think there’s a lot to like about these earnings, particularly the acceleration in revenue, improving GPU economics, and the willingness of customers to commit substantial capital upfront. But I also think the market is sending an important message to the entire AI infrastructure industry. Signing multibillion-dollar contracts is one thing. Actually delivering the computing capacity on time, managing the financing, and turning those contracts into profitable revenue is something entirely different.

Nebius has demonstrated that demand for its infrastructure is incredibly strong. Now it needs to prove that it can execute on its aggressive expansion plans without constantly running into delays and escalating costs. For long-term investors, I don’t think these results fundamentally change the AI infrastructure demand story. But they do show why execution, financing, and access to reliable power are becoming some of the biggest risks facing the neocloud industry.

原来黄仁勋突然被爆出来婚外情这事背后还有 @AnthropicAI 创始人 Dario 的参与。

而且二者之间的恩怨情仇也不是最近才结下的梁子。

早在2022 年 5 月，黄仁勋和 Dario Amodei 第一次在旧金山中餐厅 Eight Tables 里见面就因为算力订单闹红过脸。

饭前，Anthropic 负责算力的 Tom Brown 拿出一张表，说按每美元回报，谷歌自己做的加速芯片 TPU，比英伟达的 GPU 更划算。《纽约时报》记者 Kevin Roose 后来在书里写，这顿饭气氛很僵。有在场的人转述，Dario 当时嘀咕黄仁勋「有点像特朗普」。书里还写黄仁勋骂过 Brown「账房先生」，英伟达向 Roose 否认了这句。

不过，虽然那天晚上订单没有成交，但两人后来还是做成了生意，也把分歧拿到了台面上。但二人的争议焦点也随之浮出水面，那就是——先进芯片该不该卖给中国？

黄仁勋显然是有生意就做的商人，Dario则是屁股大于脑袋的派系论主义者。

2025 年 6 月，黄仁勋在巴黎说，他几乎不同意 Dario 说的所有话。他还把 Dario 的立场概括成，“AI 太可怕了，所以只有Anthropic能做AI。”

一个月后，Dario 在 Big Technology 播客里回得很冲：「这是荒谬的谎言，是我听过最荒谬的谎言。」主持人担心转述错了，Dario 说黄仁勋的原话就是这样，而且是恶意曲解。

同年 11 月，两家公司还是官宣成了战略合作伙伴。英伟达最多给 Anthropic 投 100 亿美元，微软最多投 50 亿美元，Anthropic 则承诺向微软云采购 300 亿美元算力，并先用上最多 1 吉瓦英伟达芯片，约合一座大型数据中心用电。虽然不是到手的钱，但也是实实在在的资源。

直到2025 年 3 月，Dario公开主张收紧先进芯片出口，他和黄仁勋之间的争议也逐渐暴露在公众视野之中。

2026 年 1 月，达沃斯论坛上，Dario 甚至把向国外卖先进芯片比成向朝鲜出售核武器。同月长文里他写道：「这就像把核武器卖给朝鲜，再吹嘘导弹外壳是波音造的，所以美国赢了。」

2026 年 4 月 15 日，黄仁勋上 Dwarkesh 的节目，被问到这段话。他回了一句 lunacy，也就是荒唐。

聊到美国该不该放弃中国市场时，他反问主持人：「你觉得我算美国的一部分吗？」接着他说「我们不是一辆车」，这句后来成了梗。他的意思是，计算平台不像换车那么容易，丢掉一块大市场，美国的技术优势也会松动。

今年6 月，Anthropic 称模型已经在微软云的英伟达芯片上对企业开放；11 月的上市路演里，Dario 又讲自家模型大概三天就在 AMD 芯片上跑起了推理。算力生意还在继续，但 Dario 或许早就决定，绝不把算力采购押在一家芯片上。

Swap 和发射台产生的手续费，计划全部用于回购销毁；

不发行新的子代币；

不引入质押机制；

生态收入最终回流到 MONKEY。

我个人比较认可这种思路的简单性。

很多 meme 一做生态，就开始不断增加代币、玩法和各种复杂机制，最后反而把原本的主线稀释了。

MONKEY 至少在规划上，选择了让生态围绕一个核心资产运转。

当然，规划归规划，执行是另一回事。目前官网仍处于测试阶段，回购、销毁和奖励能否持续兑现，还需要后续链上数据验证。

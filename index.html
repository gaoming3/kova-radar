Selling two options to buy one for free sounds clever until the market moves 8% and your "free" trade turns into a $4,000 margin call.

A ratio spread lets you finance a long option by selling more short contracts than you're long — for example, buying one ATM (At The Money) call on $SPY at $ and selling two OTM (Out Of The Money) calls at $ each, netting zero debit. In low Implied Volatility environments where you expect a modest directional move, this structure makes real sense: you profit if SPY drifts up moderately, and you paid nothing for the privilege. The hidden mechanic most traders miss is that beyond the short strikes, your exposure is naked — you're short one uncovered call with theoretically unlimited loss if the underlying rips through your tent structure.

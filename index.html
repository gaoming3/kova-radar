Most people either panic sell here or try to catch the exact low. Neither one is a plan.

These are the 5 signs I look for:

1. Capitulation Volume

A huge down day on heavy volume, when forced selling accelerates and investors rush for the exits.

2. Fear Spikes

The VIX jumps and the headlines turn ugly. Bottoms often form when fear is at its highest.

3. Selling Slows

Down days get smaller and selling volume starts to dry up. Fewer stocks keep making new lows.

4. Leaders Hold Up

Watch which stocks refuse to make new lows while the index does. Those are often the next leaders.

5. Follow-Through Day

A rally attempt starts, the Day 1 low holds, and on Day 4 or later, an index posts a strong gain, typically 1.25%+ on higher volume. That signals a potential new uptrend, although follow-through days can fail.

Where we are now:

⬜ 5. Follow-Through Day

Signs 1 to 4 tell you a bottom may be forming. Sign 5 provides confirmation of a rally attempt, not a guarantee that the bottom is in.

This is crazy. Someone is willing to pay me $390 to agree to buy $NVDA at $150, about 20% below where it trades today.

It's called a cash-secured put, and here's how it works:

Nvidia is trading around $188. You sell the $150 put expiring January 15, 2027 and collect $3.90 per share, or $390 for one contract.

In exchange, you agree to buy 100 shares at $150 if NVDA closes below that price at expiration. You set aside $15,000 in cash to cover it, which is why it's called "cash-secured."

The buyer is paying for the right to sell you shares at $150. The $390 is yours to keep no matter what happens.

One of two things happens:

1. NVDA stays above $150 and the put expires worthless. You keep $390 on $15,000 of capital, a 2.6% return in 70 days, or about 13.6% annualized.

2. NVDA closes below $150 and you get assigned. You buy 100 shares at $150, but after the premium your real cost basis is $146.10, about 22% below today's price.

Nvidia reports earnings after the close on November 17, before this put expires, so a big move in either direction is on the table.

The risk is real. If NVDA fell to $130, you'd be down about $1,610 on paper. Someone who bought 100 shares today would be down about $5,850.

Not financial advice. I share these for education.

That matters.

This is how market structure works:

Uptrend:

Higher highs → higher lows → trendline holds → buyers keep stepping in.

Eventually that structure breaks.

And when it does, the next phase usually isn’t:

“Straight back to all-time highs.”

It’s time.

- Time to consolidate.
- Time to build a base.
- Time to establish new support.
- Time for buyers and sellers to find equilibrium again.

Then, if the business and demand remain strong, a new uptrend can begin from that base.

This is exactly why I keep saying:

Trends don’t last forever. When one ends, let the stock rebuild before demanding the next one.

$UBER is starting to remind me a little of $GOOGL in 2024.

Google Search was supposedly about to be disrupted by AI. The stock then went on to gain ~199%.

Today, the market believes Autonomous Vehicles will disrupt Uber.

I think it may be pricing the wrong outcome.

Here’s the full $UBER thesis — valuation, AVs, Waymo, TAM and what Ackman + other top investors are seeing:

1. AVs could actually EXPAND Uber’s market

If autonomous driving lowers driver + insurance costs, rides should become cheaper.

Cheaper rides → more demand → more trips → a larger mobility TAM.

Bill Ackman made exactly this point: AVs should reduce transportation costs and therefore increase demand.

So AVs don’t necessarily have to be a zero-sum disruption.

They could make the entire market much bigger.

2. Uber doesn’t need to build the winning robotaxi

This is probably the most misunderstood part of the thesis.

Uber already has:

• 200M+ monthly users
• 40M+ trips per day
• 50M Uber One members
• a global payments layer
• dispatch + fleet positioning
• mapping data built from tens of billions of trips
• airport/event infrastructure
• regulatory relationships
• Mobility + Delivery + grocery + retail inside the same ecosystem

Uber One members already generate roughly half of Mobility + Delivery Gross Bookings.

An AV company still has to manufacture cars, finance fleets, maintain them, reposition them…and then acquire millions of customers.

Uber already has the customers.

3. We’re already seeing the model work

In Austin and Atlanta, riders can literally order a Waymo through Uber.

Uber manages and dispatches the fleet while the customer stays inside the Uber ecosystem.

And Uber isn’t betting everything on Waymo.

It has been building partnerships across https://t.co/KHyeYIesRd, WeRide, Avride, $NVDA and others. https://t.co/KHyeYIesRd is already working with Uber on international robotaxi expansion, including Europe.

That’s why I think the interesting outcome isn’t:

Uber vs. Waymo.

It’s:

Waymo + Pony + WeRide + others competing for demand INSIDE Uber.

3B. And the same playbook is coming to Uber Eats

Uber just partnered with Zipline to integrate autonomous drone delivery directly into Uber Eats. The rollout starts in Dallas and Houston by the end of 2026, with plans to expand across dozens of U.S. cities. Uber also made a strategic investment in Zipline. The long-term target is ambitious: 1 million Uber Eats drone deliveries per day by the end of 2029.

The economics could be interesting. Uber says a typical delivery takes around 30 minutes today; with Zipline, certain orders could arrive in just 5–10 minutes. Zipline’s P2 drones can carry up to 8 lbs, serve customers roughly 10 miles away, and complete a 10-mile delivery in around 10 minutes.. at a low cost.

And Zipline isn’t an early experiment anymore: it has completed 2.5M+ commercial deliveries, including roughly 1M in the last year, while the number of businesses available through its U.S. marketplace grew 13x in the first half of 2026. If faster and cheaper delivery makes people order more frequently, Uber Eats can potentially expand beyond “dinner delivery” into much more frequent grocery, convenience and everyday retail purchases.

4. Meanwhile, the core business is still compounding

Pershing Square estimates Uber can grow EPS at roughly 25% annually over the next 3–5 years.

And management entered 2026 with 200M+ monthly users, 40M+ daily trips and ~$10B of annual FCF.
Yet the market is pricing Uber like AV disruption is almost inevitable.

Using the numbers I’m looking at:

~12x 2027 FCF
~13x 2028 forward P/E

That is a very low valuation for a platform still growing earnings at anything close to Pershing’s expectations.

5. And some very smart money is clearly paying attention

Bill Ackman has made Uber one of Pershing Square’s largest positions.

David Tepper and Brad Gerstner also hold meaningful stakes.

And Nancy Pelosi’s disclosure showed 200 deep-ITM Uber calls, $50 strike, expiring March 2027, with a disclosed purchase range of $500K–$1M.

There are other notable institutional longs as well.
That doesn’t make the thesis right.

But it tells you the AV debate is far from settled.

6. And the market itself could become MUCH bigger

This is another part I think the bear case misses.

Goldman Sachs expects the global robotaxi market to reach ~$415B by 2035, with the global fleet growing from just ~7,000 vehicles today to roughly 6 million.

McKinsey estimates robotaxi cost per mile could fall 50%+ between 2025 and 2030, eventually making an autonomous ride cost just 40–50% of today’s human-driven ride-hailing trip.

Think about what that does to demand.

At sufficiently low prices, you’re not just replacing today’s Uber rides.

You start competing with car ownership, parking, rentals and potentially even public transportation for certain trips.

That means AVs could dramatically expand Uber’s TAM rather than simply redistribute the existing one.

My view:
I don’t know whether Waymo, $TSLA, or somebody else ultimately builds the best autonomous-driving technology.

I actually expect a relatively concentrated but competitive market.

What I do believe is that Uber has a very good chance of remaining a dominant distribution layer.

And even if AV adoption takes share from human drivers, Uber can still participate in that transition instead of simply getting displaced by it.

The market appears to be pricing a pretty ugly AV outcome already. (At roughly 12x 2027 FCF..)

If Uber simply keeps compounding the existing business — even somewhat slower than expected — I think today’s valuation leaves room for significant upside.

But if AVs make transportation dramatically cheaper, expand the mobility market and Uber becomes one of the dominant distribution layers connecting consumers with Waymo, Ponyai, WeRide, Tesla, Zoox and whoever else wins?

Then the thing the market currently fears most could actually become one of Uber’s biggest growth drivers.

真正值得问的是另一个问题，
它还能不能照今天这个速度继续烧钱？

这两个问题的差别，大得惊人。

一家公司不会倒闭，并不代表它的估值不会跌掉一大半；一个产业不会消失，也不代表相关股票不会经历熊市。

英伟达不肯再担保之后，那些数据中心还能不能照原计划建下去？

这些问题，现在都没有答案。

以前这个行业最关心的问题是，有没有 GPU？

现在的问题慢慢变成，谁来给 GPU 付款？

再往后，可能会变成，谁来替付款的人担保？

历史上最危险的泡沫，往往恰恰是所有人都在赚钱的时候。

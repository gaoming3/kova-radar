OPTIONS EDUCATION: Why Implied Volatility (IV) Matters?

Over the last two weeks, our SPX weekly implied volatility has increased significantly, making options much more expensive.

As an options trader, understanding IV is just as important as predicting market direction.

What is Implied Volatility?

IV represents the market’s expectation of future price movement. Higher IV means the market expects larger moves, but it does NOT tell us which direction.

Why should you care?

1. High IV: Options become expensive. Buyers pay more premium, while sellers collect more credit but face greater risk.

2. Low IV: Options are relatively cheaper, making directional buying more attractive, all else equal.

3. IV Expansion: Rising IV generally increases option premiums, benefiting buyers who are already holding positions.

4. IV Crush: Falling IV reduces option premiums. You can predict the direction correctly and STILL lose money!

My Trading Approach:

When IV is elevated, I become more selective with entries, position sizing, and strike selection. Sometimes you can prefer defined-risk debit spreads instead of buying expensive single-leg options.

Remember, being right about direction is not enough. You also need to understand what you are paying for that direction.

Price tells you WHERE the market is going. IV tells you HOW MUCH the market expects it to move.

Learn both before risking your money.

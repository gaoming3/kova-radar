One of the most important moving averages I keep an eye on:

The 50 week EMA...

If you trade momentum, learn to pay attention to this level.

Why?

Because some of the strongest stocks in the market can spend months riding the 50 week EMA during their biggest Stage 2 advances

A stock can have multiple red weeks.

It can pull back 50%+

It can look “weak” on the daily chart.

But when you zoom out to the weekly?

The primary momentum trend may have never been broken.

That’s where the 50 week EMA becomes extremely useful.

I use it for 3 main things:

1. Identifying powerful trends

When a stock continues making higher highs and higher lows while consistently respecting a rising 50-week EMA, that's usually a sign of very strong momentum.

The strongest leaders don't always give you deep pullbacks.

They move higher → consolidate → test the 50 week → and continue.

Almost like a staircase.

2. Separating a normal pullback from a potential character change

Not every red candle means the trend is over.

When a leader pulls back, zoom out.

Is the weekly structure still intact?

Is the 50 week EMA still rising?

Is price still respecting it?

Is selling volume decreasing into the pullback?

If so, what looks ugly on the daily chart can simply be the stock digesting its previous move.

This is why timeframe matters.

3. Finding where momentum could potentially reset

Eventually, even the strongest stocks become extended.

I don't want to chase a stock that's trading vertically away from its moving averages.

I'd rather see:

Most of the leading stocks in the market end up doing this:

Expansion --> Contraction --> 50 week EMA catches up --> Volume dries up --> New setup forms

That reset can create a beautiful weekly continuation and momentum will starts coming back in to the trade

The amount of previous leaders setting up off the 50 week right is incredible

The one thing I never hear from the $UBER bulls is how they will compete given Tesla’s unit economics advantage.

Cybercab will be lower cost to manufacture, lower cost to operate, and also offers a better customer experience with the interior of the cab. This will directly translate to the lowest cost to operate, and therefore to the lowest customer price per mile.

So while I acknowledge the value of Uber’s network and accomplishments to date, I can’t see them competing profitably under any reasonable set of scenarios.

And the moat of their network strikes me as extremely fragile. It wasn’t long ago that most Uber drivers I spoke with also drove for Lyft, and they would take rides from whichever platform gave them the best earnings profile, often switching multiple times throughout a single day. As a consumer I did the same, until somewhat recently as Uber pulled away.

If I’m a rider needing to choose between Uber and a Tesla Robotaxi, it’s true that I’m more familiar with the Uber and at least initially theres likely to be some stasis that prevents an immediate switch. But at sone point, all I will have to do is download the Robotaxi app and see that my rides are meaningfully cheaper with this option.

So I’ll give it a whirl, and then I’ll realize that on top of being cheaper, it’s also more comfortable, more private, and an overall superior customer experience.

Uber does have its advantages in this fight, but they are not durable advantages.

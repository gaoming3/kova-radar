The bond market is no longer the safe haven it once was.

The one thing I never hear from the $UBER bulls is how they will compete given Tesla’s unit economics advantage.

Cybercab will be lower cost to manufacture, lower cost to operate, and also offers a better customer experience with the interior of the cab. This will directly translate to the lowest cost to operate, and therefore to the lowest customer price per mile.

So while I acknowledge the value of Uber’s network and accomplishments to date, I can’t see them competing profitably under any reasonable set of scenarios.

And the moat of their network strikes me as extremely fragile. It wasn’t long ago that most Uber drivers I spoke with also drove for Lyft, and they would take rides from whichever platform gave them the best earnings profile, often switching multiple times throughout a single day. As a consumer I did the same, until somewhat recently as Uber pulled away.

If I’m a rider needing to choose between Uber and a Tesla Robotaxi, it’s true that I’m more familiar with the Uber and at least initially theres likely to be some stasis that prevents an immediate switch. But at sone point, all I will have to do is download the Robotaxi app and see that my rides are meaningfully cheaper with this option.

So I’ll give it a whirl, and then I’ll realize that on top of being cheaper, it’s also more comfortable, more private, and an overall superior customer experience.

Uber does have its advantages in this fight, but they are not durable advantages.

Two of the world’s strongest consumer brands are losing billions in market value and the reasons go far beyond a weak quarter.

Nike and Lululemon are facing a deeper problem, changing consumer preferences, tougher competition, weaker pricing power, and product decisions that are starting to catch up with them.

Here is what went wrong:

Nike ($NKE) | Market Cap: $57B
Down roughly 50% from its 52-week high and after losing more than $220B in peak value, Nike is facing one of the biggest challenges in its history.
➜ Nike aggressively cut ties with long-term wholesale partners like Foot Locker to push its Direct-to-Consumer strategy, but the move weakened critical retail channels.
➜ Fast-moving competitors like Hoka, On Running, and Salomon capitalised on Nike's stagnation and rapidly gained market share.
➜ Nike is now focused on clearing older inventory and creating room for newer, more innovative products as it tries to rebuild momentum.

Lululemon ($LULU) | Market Cap: $11B
The premium athleisure giant has fallen more than 50% this year, wiping out billions in shareholder value and raising serious questions about its growth model.

➜ Its signature leggings category saw sales fall around 20%, showing that weakness has reached one of its most important product categories.
➜ Product launches and design issues have hurt customer loyalty, while recalls and fit concerns have added pressure to the brand.
➜ Management has now cut its full-year revenue and earnings outlook, leaving the incoming CEO with a major turnaround challenge.

Brand loyalty can disappear much faster than investors expect. When consumer preferences shift and competitors move faster, even the strongest names in retail can lose their edge.

按这个收益率倒推，这一大笔仓位的综合成本大约只有 44 万美元。更夸张的是，他根本不只买 STONK：$CLANKER 大约 9.3 万美元市值就进了，4500 美元一度变成 24.8 万。

$PURR 在约 11.4 万市值买，3500 美元做到 7.6 万；$FRIES 在约 13.6 万市值埋伏，后来做到约 34 倍。

然后是 $ZCAT，这笔更能看懂他的第二阶段打法。

9 月 5 日开始买，第二天 FOMO 统计时已经持有约 690 万枚，价值 62.9 万美元、收益 +439%。

现在已经变成 720 万枚、价值约 120 万美元、收益 +699%。也就是说，他不是发现涨了以后梭哈，而是先在没人注意的时候拿主仓，等叙事开始发酵以后还在继续买。

最有意思的是后半段。仓位已经赚几十倍之后，他仍然会出现很多 10 美元、20 美元、几百美元这种几乎不影响仓位的小额追加，同时 X 上开始不断聊 STONK、Solana、Stocks onchain。

单看金额毫无意义，但放到 FOMO 这种每笔交易本身就是内容的社交交易平台里，意义完全变了：

每一次小买单都会重新把榜一还在买这个信号推给关注他的人。

最近他公开写过STONK is really about to save SOL，今天又直接说Stocks onchain… is crypto’s true growing up moment。

所以我现在觉得 @pointfarmcap 最值得研究的是这套交易顺序：

先发现一个可能成为 Meta 的生态 → 在十几万、几十万市值把生态批量买一遍 → 跑出龙头后把真正的仓位集中到赢家 → 叙事发酵后继续用小额交易保持 FOMO Feed 曝光，同时公开强化自己的判断。

这就解释了为什么他账户有 60 个持仓，现金却只剩 2.88 美元。这个人不是在排行榜里找币，他是在排行榜出现之前买币。

这篇 77 页 TradFi Playbook 最核心的东西：不是产品变多了，而是同一笔资产开始有了不同的“状态”。

以前看到 Binance 上合约、股票、bStocks、股票期权，可能会觉得只是不断增加产品线。

但如果把它们放在一起看，逻辑完全不一样：

TradFi 永续 = 表达观点
Direct Stocks = 获得所有权
bStocks = 获得链上流动性和可组合性
Options = 定义风险

它们并不是互相替代，而是在解决同一笔敞口不同阶段的问题。

比如同样看好 $NVDA：

长期看好，可以拿 Direct Stocks；

短期想放大方向判断，可以用永续；

财报/FOMC 前担心尾部风险，可以用 Put 给仓位上保险；

想获得更灵活的链上流动性，又可以切到 bStocks。

最重要的变化，是“资产”和“交易工具”开始被放进同一套资金体系里。

以前你可能要在券商、交易所、钱包之间反复转钱；现在 Binance 想做的，是让稳定币成为统一入口。

让一笔资金在持有—交易—对冲—上链之间切换。

大老师原文甚至把它概括成：这四种产品更像是“同一笔敞口在不同生命周期里的四种状态”。

所以 说“真正有意思的是这些产品开始连起来”，我觉得这句话才是整篇最值得看的地方。

普通人看到的是 Binance 多了几个 TradFi 产品；真正懂产品的人看到的是：

Crypto 和 TradFi 原本割裂的账户、流动性和风险管理体系，正在被重新拼到一起。

能把一份 77 页的硬核 Playbook 提炼到这条主线上，YY 这篇总结得确实漂亮。

也推荐大家把 @octopusycc 大老师的原文一起看完，产品逻辑讲得非常透。

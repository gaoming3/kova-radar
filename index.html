Where there's one cockroach, there are usually many. And $NVDA is starting to look like a building where someone just turned on the lights on its incredibly poor governance.

CBS just reported Chinese-controlled companies gaining access to Nvidia's advanced chips through U.S. entities. One Nvidia partner reportedly exported $5.6 billion in advanced technology to Southeast Asia, including $3 billion in restricted Blackwell systems..Then there's the $7.9 billion in additional Chinese-headquartered customer billings revealed through accounting changes.

Meanwhile, the WSJ also just reported an executive relationship involving a China-linked company, raising further questions about corporate governance.

None of this establishes wrongdoing by Nvidia, but it raises some uncomfortable questions especially for a company of its size and importance.

The problem with poor corporate governance is that the damage rarely happens overnight. It builds quietly beneath the surface, often hidden by strong earnings, rising share prices and investor enthusiasm. The damage is done when it becomes embedded into a dysfunctional and potentially toxic culture of inappropriate behaviour at the top of the organization.

When the cracks finally begin to appear, investors discover that the risks were far greater than they imagined. What initially looks like an isolated incident can quickly become a much broader systemic problem involving regulatory scrutiny, reputational damage and declining investor confidence.

This is where investor psychology becomes dangerous. Investors anchor themselves to previous highs, dismiss warning signs and convince themselves every selloff is a buying opportunity.

But the biggest mistake is assuming you've found the last cockroach simply because you've discovered the first few.
I'd rather preserve capital and wait for the exterminators and right now, we're still discovering what's really hiding behind the walls.

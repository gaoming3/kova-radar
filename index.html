If your employer is offering you a buyout, let me explain what is happening a bit....

And before I get into $MSFT Microsoft, here's something I've been telling people for years.

Start treating every job like a temporary job.

I don't care how long you've been there, how good your reviews are, or how much your boss loves you. Keep your resume current. Keep your network alive. Know what you're worth outside the company. And always know where your next opportunity might come from.

You don't have to be actively interviewing every week. But you should never be starting from zero when your employer decides it's time for you to go.

It's a hell of a lot better to leave on your own terms than to find yourself negotiating an exit you never saw coming.

As for the buyouts... companies don't put voluntary exit programs in front of thousands of employees because they're feeling generous.

They're trying to reduce headcount. Voluntarily is cheaper, cleaner, and considerably less disruptive than forcing people out.

And here's what employees consistently underestimate...the company has already made a decision about its workforce. You're still trying to decide whether it includes you. STOP right there.

There have been numerous buyouts in 2026... the Microsoft one is the latest and biggest... and they are offering buyouts to roughly 60,000 employees. That doesn't mean 60,000 layoffs are coming. (well I think it will be close on exits over the next 6-8 months) BUT it does mean that management has opened the door to a workforce reduction on a massive scale.

If too few people walk through it, management still has other options.

As Mark mentioned below... he has seen too many executives convince themselves they're too valuable, too experienced, too connected, or too important to lose. Then they're absolutely stunned when their position disappears. This hits at all levels, not just exec too.

Your tenure is not a protection plan. Your title is not a protection plan. And your relationship with leadership is certainly not a protection plan.

My advice? TAKE THE DAMN BUYOUT. Review the terms, your equity, healthcare, and what you'd receive if you waited. Because the worst time to start negotiating your future is after your employer has decided it no longer needs your job.

And please stop confusing being valuable to a company with being indispensable to its next strategy. Those are two very different things.

Serious question: How many of you would turn down a buyout because you genuinely believe your company couldn't afford to lose you?

这意味着随着Data Center业务规模扩大，AMD已经能够把收入增长转化成更快的营业利润增长，这部分我认为非常重要，因为最终我们还是希望AI需求能够带来实际的EPS增长

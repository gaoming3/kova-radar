Are they moving in one direction? Or bouncing between the same support and resistance?

Stop expecting a trend day when price keeps giving you a range.

2. MARK YOUR LEVELS BEFORE THE OPEN.

• Previous day’s high and low
• Premarket high and low
• Previous close
• Major daily and weekly support/resistance

Make the plan BEFORE price gets there.

3. STOP CHASING THE MIDDLE.

At support: watch for buyers to defend it.

At resistance: watch for sellers to reject it.

In the middle: WAIT.

Buying after a big bounce can put you straight into resistance. Shorting after a big drop can put you straight into support.

4. WAIT FOR CONFIRMATION.

Break -> Retest -> Enter.

Long: break above a level, come back and hold.

Short: break below a level, bounce back and fail.

Use the 8/21 to help confirm direction. A cross means little if the next major level is right in front of you.

No retest, no trade.

5. TRADE TO THE NEXT LEVEL.

Know your target and stop BEFORE entering.

Not enough room to justify the risk? Skip it.

On range days, take profits as price approaches the next level.

If we break through and hold, THEN look for continuation.

Stop turning a winning trade into a loser because you wanted a bigger move.

KEEP YOURSELF IN THE GAME.

Size so you can accept the loss.

Don’t move your stop.
Don’t add to a loser.
Don’t double your size to make it back.

Hit your daily loss limit? Shut it down.

News and data can start the move. Price at your levels tells you whether there’s a trade.

You don’t need to predict the whole day.

Trade level to level. Get paid. Wait for the next setup.

We went back through 35 midterm elections dating to 1886 to study how the market performed when Democrats won versus Republicans.

The results deserve a closer look. 📊

For the main comparison, “winning” means securing the House majority, whether a party gained control or kept it. We also tested Senate control and split Congress outcomes separately.

Here’s what the S&P averaged after the 19 midterms from 1950 through 2022:

🔵 DEMOCRATIC HOUSE OUTCOME
• Next 2 months: +3.7%
• Next 1 year: +15.4%
• Next 2 years: +25.2%
• Next 3 years: +32.4%
• Next 4 years: +33.1%
🔴 REPUBLICAN HOUSE OUTCOME
• Next 2 months: +3.0%
• Next 1 year: +14.4%
• Next 2 years: +31.9%
• Next 3 years: +47.1%
• Next 4 years: +55.9%

The most consistent finding:
The market finished higher one year after EVERY midterm in that modern sample.
Democratic House outcomes: 13 out of 13.
Republican House outcomes: 6 out of 6.

That is a historical observation. It does not give the next election a 100% probability of a positive one-year return.
There’s also a major detail behind that Republican four-year average.

The four years following the 1994 midterm produced approximately +145%. Remove that single election, and the Republican average falls from +55.9% to +33.6%—very close to the Democratic average of +33.1%.
One election makes an enormous difference when the sample contains only five completed four-year outcomes.
The path matters, too.

After the 2018 midterm, the market was DOWN 8.1% at the two-month mark, yet finished UP 11.7% after one year. That first year included a 16.4% peak-to-trough closing drawdown.

A bullish historical average can still come with a difficult trading environment.
For this year’s Republican-president setup, the modern comparison is especially limited. Since 1950, there were nine midterms resulting in a Democratic House under a Republican president, but only ONE resulting in a Republican House: 2002.

That deserves caution before anyone presents either outcome as a dependable market forecast.

Our research found favorable average returns following both parties’ House victories. The size of the apparent party advantage changes with the historical period, the horizon, and individual outliers.

And here's another major development: Rocket Lab has fully financed its pending acquisition of Iridium Communications, completing a $1.94 BILLION equity offering. If completed, the acquisition would give Rocket Lab its own global satellite communications network, transforming it into a company capable of building, launching, and operating satellite constellations.

Through its acquisitions, Rocket Lab also owns technology already proven on NASA's Perseverance rover on Mars. Its expanding Space Systems business now supplies critical satellite components, robotics, and communications technologies across the industry.

Yes, Neutron's inaugural launch has been delayed until 2027. But management has identified the manufacturing issue involving its carbon-composite structures and successfully tested a revised manufacturing process on one of the rocket's domes. Further testing remains, but the delay doesn't erase the progress across the rest of the business.

Think about the setup: revenue growing 62%, Space Systems growing 94%, a $2.36B backlog, record commercial launch contracts, expanding government business, and a major satellite communications acquisition in the works.

Yet $RKLB is trading around $51.

The space economy isn't just about launching rockets. It needs satellites, communications networks, spacecraft components, and the infrastructure that keeps everything operating. Rocket Lab is positioning itself to become a major provider of that critical infrastructure.

At around $51, I strongly believe $RKLB offers one of the most compelling long-term opportunities in the space sector for investors willing to look beyond short term setbacks and accept the execution risks.

$IXIC has broken key weekly support, and $SPX has fallen below its MA20 again. Should we buy or sell?

The IVTS indicator is giving us a signal.

IVTS (Implied Volatility Term Structure) is the ratio of 30-day implied volatility (VIX) to 3-month implied volatility (VIX3M).

When IVTS rises above 1.0, it signals backwardation—short-term implied volatility exceeds longer-term implied volatility, reflecting heightened near-term market fear.

Looking at the chart comparing $SPX and IVTS over the past two years, every time IVTS approached or exceeded 1.0, U.S. equity indices were near a relative market bottom.

Combining this signal with my macroeconomic outlook and forward-looking analysis of the AI industry, I believe the probability of U.S. stocks entering an outright bear market remains very low.

Amid uncertainty surrounding the U.S.–Iran conflict and the midterm elections, the market will likely find its bottom through continued volatility and consolidation.

My conclusion: Over the next three weeks, from tomorrow through early November, I'll systematically accumulate U.S. equities and scale into high-quality stocks on dips.

$BE’s CEO just made some HUGE predictions about the future of power during the company's earnings call.

One of the biggest takeaways was how confident management is about the future of on site power generation. The CEO essentially argued that the traditional electrical grid was never designed to handle the enormous, concentrated power demands we’re seeing from AI data centers. Hyperscalers are increasingly realizing that waiting years for grid connections isn’t realistic, which is why they’re turning to companies like Bloom to generate electricity directly at their facilities. What I found particularly interesting is that management believes this shift will eventually extend far beyond AI data centers. Factories, hospitals, military installations, commercial buildings and even entire international markets could increasingly move toward generating their own electricity. The CEO compared this transition to developing countries skipping landline telephones and moving directly to mobile phones.

Management also made it clear that they’re already preparing for demand beyond 5 GW of annual manufacturing capacity. They aren’t committing to a specific timeline, but the CEO said he doesn’t see them dialing down production expansion anytime soon. The goal is to increase capacity alongside customer demand without becoming the bottleneck preventing projects from moving forward. Speaking of bottlenecks, there was an interesting update on Nebius $NBIS. Bloom confirmed that its equipment is already ready for the first phase of Nebius’s 300 MW New Jersey data center. Management emphasized that they will have power available as Nebius brings additional capacity online, meaning Bloom does not expect to be the reason the project gets delayed.

The CEO also referenced Jensen Huang’s comments at GTC Berlin, where Jensen reportedly said that every quarter of delay for a 1 GW AI data center could translate into $10 billion in missed revenue. Think about what that means. When hyperscalers are potentially losing billions because they cannot bring compute online quickly enough, the speed at which a company can deliver electricity becomes incredibly valuable. Another important development was Bloom’s growing relationship with utilities. Historically, the company has largely sold on site power directly to customers. Now, utilities are beginning to recognize that installing Bloom’s fuel cells throughout their own networks could be faster and more economical than waiting for massive transmission and generation projects. Management believes this could become a major growth opportunity because utilities could purchase systems in anticipation of future electricity demand rather than waiting for individual customers. Carbon capture was another major topic. Bloom highlighted its 700 MW project involving Google and AEP, which will use fuel cells alongside carbon capture to provide near zero carbon electricity. Management explained that its technology produces a more concentrated stream of CO2 than conventional combustion systems, potentially making carbon capture easier and more economical. The CEO believes this could eventually become a trillion dollar market opportunity, and said hyperscalers are already asking about making their systems carbon-capture ready. There was also a discussion around 800V DC power delivery, which I think is worth paying attention to. Today, data centers lose efficiency converting electricity multiple times before it reaches the actual AI servers. Bloom believes its technology could eliminate some of those conversions, potentially saving billions in operating costs. Management is already having conversations with hyperscalers and expects adoption to accelerate as newer server architectures become available.

Overall, my biggest takeaway is that Bloom is positioning itself as much more than a fuel cell manufacturer. Management sees the company becoming a major part of how electricity is generated and delivered, especially as AI, automation, and electrification continue pushing power demand higher. I thought the call was very encouraging, particularly the comments around manufacturing expansion, Nebius, utilities, and the growing demand for on site power. The big question now is how effectively Bloom can execute as the scale of these projects continues increasing. Yes, $BE is down nearly 7% after hours despite reporting an extremely strong quarter, but I think a lot of this has to do with the current market environment we’re in. High growth stocks are getting punished across the board, regardless of how well their underlying businesses are performing. I’m not going to pretend I know where the bottom is, but my long term conviction hasn’t changed. Once the broader market stabilizes and investors become more comfortable taking risks again, I believe $BE will recover as well. The fundamentals are moving in the right direction, and that’s what matters most to me.

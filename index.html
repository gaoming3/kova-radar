QQQ had closed below its 20 week SMA while SPY was still holding above its own.

I explained that this was a serious warning sign based on a pattern I’ve observed during previous corrections.

When QQQ breaks its 20 week SMA and fails to reclaim it, SPY often follows. Once SPY also loses that weekly support, the risk of a deeper correction increases significantly.

That’s exactly what happened.

QQQ failed to reclaim its 20 week SMA, SPY eventually broke below its own, and the market suffered a brutal sell off.

This wasn’t about predicting the exact day of the collapse. It was about recognising the warning signs before the damage became obvious.

2. Why I expect another test of $727–726

SPY spent most of today’s session struggling to reclaim its first resistance around $741.

To me, this looks more like a dead cat bounce than a convincing bullish reversal.

I still expect a retest of yesterday’s overnight low near $727.41, potentially next week.

That area corresponds roughly to 7313 on SPX, around the July lows, an important liquidity zone from which the market previously rallied to new highs.

It’s also where SPY’s 200 day EMA and SMA are clustered, around $730–726.

That’s my preferred zone for another bullish swing.

$718 is also an important level where the 50 weekly SMA is sitting. If $726 fails $718 will be the next major support for the bounce.

3. Why I’m not buying puts here ?

Even though I expect further downside, I’m not interested in chasing shorts here.

The 4 hour RSI is around 30–35, much closer to my preferred conditions for buying calls.

Historically, my best swing shorts have come with the 4 hour RSI above 70, offering better risk/reward.

SPY can certainly fall further without that condition, but shorting after such extreme selling isn’t attractive to me.

4. What would change my mind?

I’d want SPY to reclaim and hold $741, followed by $744–745, before reconsidering whether the bottom is already in.

Today’s close offered no convincing confirmation. It was actually terrible.

5. Is this becoming a bear market?

I’m not convinced.

Technology, semiconductors and AI-related stocks have suffered particularly severe corrections, but I still see this as a correction rather than automatically assuming a prolonged bear market.

Extreme volatility spikes and heavy selling volume can sometimes signal short-term capitulation.

I believe selling pressure may be approaching exhaustion, although I don’t think the final low is necessarily behind us.

6. My November outlook

My preferred scenario remains a retest of $727–726, maybe even $718.

$765 is my initial major target, with a possible extension towards $771.

If $726 breaks decisively, the next major support is the 50 week SMA near $718.

Even if we test $718 first, I still expect a meaningful recovery afterwards.

But a strong relief rally doesn’t automatically mean the broader correction is over.

For stronger bullish confirmation, QQQ needs to reclaim its 20 week SMA.

That’s the same indicator I warned about before this decline, and I’ll continue respecting it.

Bottom line:

I closed my small calls position at $739 for a small profit and finished the week entirely in cash.

I’m not chasing puts after the collapse, and I’m not buying calls into a weak bounce.

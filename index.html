Today was a clear wake-up call for the market. It’s apparent that most investors weren’t, and perhaps still aren’t, prepared for each of the big 5 (incl. SPCX) to potentially raise multiple hundreds of billions of equity/debt capital through 2030, and multiple trillions collectively. These investors also weren’t prepared for the capex growth to have no end in sight.

Ironically, many of the executives of these companies have been out in public clearly communicating their intention to keep spending very aggressively, with no plans to stop anytime soon.

And yet, for whatever reason, street estimates called for capex to slow dramatically into 2028 and basically plateau thereafter. The street modeled 50-60% capex growth across the big 5 in 2027 (which itself is low), and then MSD% growth annually. There is no good fundamental reason to model that unless you think companies spend less because the returns on the technology slow, or you think there are severe physical/power/financing constraints (though this would just result in slower growth, not the plateau that’s been modeled).

But it seems just as likely that out-year estimates simply defaulted to a plateau. Companies only guide a year out, and few on the sell-side are willing to model years of cash burn at this scale, so in their models, burn peaks in 2027 before FCF flips back positive in 2028 and scales from there. That path doesn’t require nearly as much capital issuance. To be clear, this would be a much happier and easier world for equity investors, who could go back to reaping the rewards instead of writing the checks. But for those who took the companies at their word and modeled continued elevated capex growth through the balance of this decade, these levels of issuance were a mathematical inevitability.

Yesterday’s filing from $META probably forced many to contemplate for the first time that each of these companies could actually burn and issue multiple hundreds of billions of dollars in the coming years. The credit markets have already shown signs of strain under the sheer magnitude of issuance, so more of this may need to be done through equity. That means there was also some front-running of eventual, unknown but likely material levels of dilution.

Equity markets don’t like being diluted, and they generally don’t like companies spending money at all. Especially not when the dollars get this big and when, rightly or wrongly, many still doubt the viability or magnitude of the returns, or at the very least don’t care to wait around to find out. Add in the crowding and leverage, along with a continued rise in oil on Middle East flare-ups, and you get a meaningful stock correction.

And so that’s what I believe the market priced in today: more burn, more issuance, more dilution, more leverage, more risk, more financing constraints (esp. for the most rate-exposed stuff, which got hit the worst today), along with some uptick in the chance this all falls apart.

This is going to sound perverse, but I think a day like today is very healthy. The companies are ambitious and the markets are stingy. And they will have to work with one another to get what they want. The companies want unprecedented capital to invest in what they believe is a re-platforming of the entire global infrastructure for an AI future, and the markets demand sufficient evidence that they’ll earn above the required return on the capital they lay out and the risk they take on. Ultimately, this forces the companies to be more disciplined and to show ROI as early and visibly as possible. That doesn’t mean they spend less, just that they have to prove the spend is working. I don’t think Zuck’s comment about monetizing Muse so much more rapidly than previous platforms was a coincidence. He knows what he needs, and he knows what he needs to show to get it.

So what now? Is AI a powerful enough driver to muscle its way through the fears being expressed by markets?

I think so. The big 5 may grow capex from >$1.3 trillion in 2027 to >$2.5 trillion in 2030, a ~25% CAGR. That implies a lot of paper, but in general, the hyperscalers can afford to pay higher rates, and there still seems to be demand from credit investors for it. At yesterday’s Robin Hood conference, execs at Diameter and HPS expressed a view that 7-8.5% yields on bonds from these companies are interesting. And I’d argue that’s for good reason: aside from SPCX, these companies generate massive operating cash flow and have largely benign net debt/EBITDA levels.

On the other hand, the more speculative companies that sit further out on the risk curve, pay higher spreads, and often don’t generate any cash at all will likely be forced to spend less. If you thought CRWV and NBIS would spend a combined ~$150-200 billion of capex in 2030 (vs. ~$60 billion in 2026), that might get cut in half because of financing constraints. But even a $100 billion reduction is only a 3-4% hit relative to the big 5’s 2030 capex, which, on net, is very manageable. That may be an issue for those companies, but it’s not enough to derail the ecosystem as a whole.

Ultimately, you have to believe there are attractive returns in AI, which I strongly believe are inflecting and wrote about here: https://t.co/53umFQdntz.

So even while things remain turbulent, the path still appears bright.

I've held $ASTS since the de-SPAC and bought everywhere from $2 to over $100. Now I'm looking hard at the opportunity cost and asking whether my money is in the right place for what's next. I don't think I'm alone.

Some of the drop is macro, a soft sector, and fading momentum. But relative to the rest of the sector, we do struggle with communication and dare I say, credibility. Timeline after timeline from earnings calls has slipped: satellites in orbit, commercial service, and the government contract details we were told were coming. Some of this has been out of our control - but communication is always available to management.

Management, here's what long-term holders need on the next call:

1️⃣ Production: How many BlueBirds were completed in each of July, August, and September? On what date will Midland hit a sustained 6 per month? Investors need to gauge production process. If we cannot meet 6 per month, then all future financial projections should be called into question.

2️⃣ Launches: Give us the launch-by-launch schedule through mid-2027, with the target month, and the number of satellites on each one. Which are under signed contract, and which are still tentative?

3️⃣ Government: Name the government agreements that are actually signed, their dollar value, and how long they run. Which of the contracts that were touted are still pending, and by what date will they be disclosed?

4️⃣ Capital: In July you raised $1.15B in convertible notes. You said the money would "secure additional access to orbit" and fund "partnerships and/or acquisitions to further vertically integrate" and reduce reliance on third-party launch providers, and you also said there were no agreements yet. Three months later, what launch access has that money actually secured, and what vertical-integration deals have been signed? How much of the $1.15B has been spent, and on what? With over $3.7B in pro forma cash, what's the quarterly burn through 2027, and is another raise coming?

In this market we don't need more optimism. We need numbers, dates, and commitments that get met. I believe that the mission of this company is going to change lives, create a ton of value, and is in the end quite noble.

I understand space is hard and expensive and timelines can shift. In the last few years I have learned more about spectrum, wavelengths, and regulatory requirements than I ever thought I would. I believe the technology is superior to anything else available and that the first principles approach has been dead on from the beginning.

That being said, we are competing in a field with the richest and most successful business men ever to have lived. The odds are certainly stacked against us, and timing here is everything. I think Space growth is going to be a defining shift in the trajectory of humanity akin to the railroad, international shipping, computing, and the internet. This is a huge opportunity that generations after us will wish they had a chance to invest in - but I have to ask whether I have backed the right horse at the right time. I believe I have, but I need more from our leadership at this pivotal moment.

Hold tight, Spacemob. 🛰️

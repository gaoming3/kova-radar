Elon Musk: "By the end of this year, chip production will outpace the ability to turn chips on."

I doubt most investors understand the profundity of this statement and its implications for cloud providers like $IREN.

If chip production (hardware supply) does outpace the ability to turn chips on (hardware demand), due to power constraints, how do you think that plays out for semi margins and for the hardware expenses cloud providers carry?

The forces of supply and demand would dictate that margins compress and hardware prices come down.

That carries major implications for the broader AI industry.

For one, the earnings power of many semis could be in the process of peaking.

But the more interesting implication for investors in the likes of $IREN is that falling AI hardware costs mean a lower relative capex burden, and thus lower depreciation, leading to expanding profit margins.

My honest read on Elon's comment is that he may be early by a year or two, but I firmly believe this is the direction we're heading.

In fact, I believe we're heading into a multi-year period I've coined the "golden age of neoclouds," in which cloud margins expand as a direct result of this phenomenon.

The golden age is obviously only 'golden' for those able to take advantage of falling hardware prices, meaning those able to actually plug the chips in.

In other words, the primary beneficiaries will be the clouds that secured access to power early and can leverage it to expand their data center footprint, while competitors sit stuck at the bottleneck.

With one of the world's largest and fastest growing secured power portfolios at 5.8 GW, up nearly 3 GW in only 6 months, my money is on $IREN.

Jason Luongo@JasonL_Capital10h
This is crazy. Someone is willing to pay me $390 to agree to buy $NVDA at $150, about 20% below where it trades today.

It's called a cash-secured put, and here's how it works:

Nvidia is trading around $188. You sell the $150 put expiring January 15, 2027 and collect $3.90 per share, or $390 for one contract.

In exchange, you agree to buy 100 shares at $150 if NVDA closes below that price at expiration. You set aside $15,000 in cash to cover it, which is why it's called "cash-secured."

The buyer is paying for the right to sell you shares at $150. The $390 is yours to keep no matter what happens.

One of two things happens:

1. NVDA stays above $150 and the put expires worthless. You keep $390 on $15,000 of capital, a 2.6% return in 70 days, or about 13.6% annualized.

2. NVDA closes below $150 and you get assigned. You buy 100 shares at $150, but after the premium your real cost basis is $146.10, about 22% below today's price.

Nvidia reports earnings after the close on November 17, before this put expires, so a big move in either direction is on the table.

The risk is real. If NVDA fell to $130, you'd be down about $1,610 on paper. Someone who bought 100 shares today would be down about $5,850.

Not financial advice. I share these for education.

$ASTS: 🚨CLEAR STREET REITERATES BUY RATING AND $115 PRICE TARGET

AST Moves Toward Full Integration

Summary

While the market is overly focused on launch bottlenecks, AST's announcement of its own reusable 20-ton rocket, Blacklift, moves it toward full vertical integration and addresses the longer-term concern. Powered by Firefly Aerospace's (NASDAQ: FLY, NC) Miranda and Vira engines, Blacklift targets first flights in late 2028 with up to five BlueBirds per launch. Key points heading into 3Q26 earnings Monday, November 9, after the close:

Full integration: Blacklift would complete AST's end-to-end model
Near-term relief: Watch for AST's 2027E–2028E launch outlook
Sovereignty: Expect updates on sovereign deals as governments seek network control.
Spectrum: Separate from earnings, FCC action on Ligado's pending SkyTerra Next application could come at any time.

We reiterate our Buy rating and $115 price target (20x 2029E adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.1B vs. ~6x at $30 today).

Key Points
Full integration costs ~$1.3B. Blacklift would complete AST's end-to-end model and cut reliance on Falcon 9, with SpaceX (SPCX, Buy) reportedly turning away new bookings beyond 2028 (Bloomberg). The Firefly deal includes ~$800M upfront ($400M cash; 10–14M Class A shares) for a perpetual engine license, $250M for the first three rockets' engines, and $250M for three years of engineering support. That is ~$900M in cash and ~3% dilution, vs. $3.7B in pro forma 2Q26 cash and restricted cash.

Near-term relief depends on two rockets returning. ULA's Vulcan targets a return to flight Friday, November 13, after a nine-month grounding. A successful flight would indirectly help AST by freeing Falcon 9 capacity, though AST has not publicly confirmed Vulcan contracts. A December New Glenn return would restore AST's high-capacity option at up to eight BlueBirds per launch.

Sovereignty is a growing tailwind. Vodafone's (NASDAQ: VOD, NC) Tim Boddy said at Network X in Vienna that the AST venture is a stronger sovereign option than Starlink or Amazon, as its core network and radio infrastructure sit in Europe under the Luxembourg JV deal (Light Reading).

Spectrum is the opportunity the market is missing. AST's Ligado agreement gives it 80+ years of rights to up to 45 MHz (40 MHz of L-band) for $550M at closing, pending FCC approval. SkyTerra Next would put L-band payloads on 96 AST satellites. Iridium (IRDM, Neutral) opposes, alleging spectrum hoarding; Ligado disputes it. At 45 MHz x 340M POPs x $1.10 per MHz-pop (SpaceX paid ~$1.00 for EchoStar's 50 MHz, ~$17B), approval implies up to $16.8B in value, vs. an ~$11.7B market cap at $30.
